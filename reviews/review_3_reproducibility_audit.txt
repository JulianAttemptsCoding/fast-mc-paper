## Bottom line

This is a **good internal validation note**, but it is **not yet defensible as a final fast-simulation paper**. The draft is unusually candid about negative results and test-set history, which is useful. But after several passes, I see a handful of issues that are serious enough that I would stop before polishing prose.

The single biggest red flag is the **condition-only C2ST AUROC of 0.4636**. If the generated and Geant4 classes really use the *same 10,000-condition multiset*, a classifier given only those conditions should see identical empirical feature distributions. A result materially away from 0.5 means either the evaluator folds destroy the pairing, the two classes do not actually have identical condition sets, preprocessing differs, or something in the evaluator is wrong. Until that is understood, I would not trust the quantitative interpretation of Fig. 2. The manuscript currently calls 0.4636 “near chance.” 

The other major blockers are: the unknown relationship between the pilot bank and canonical validation split; the partially compromised historical test split; potentially incomplete conditioning on the neutron state; insufficient specification of Geant4/data generation; absence of M0-fresh **physics diagnostics** for the architecture screens; unproven “guarantees” around finite/positive floating-point outputs; almost no statistical uncertainty; and calling the model “fast” before any timing exists. Your own abstract/conclusion explicitly says fidelity and speedup are not established, which conflicts somewhat with the title.

# Audit pass 1 — claim/evidence consistency

The paper is internally much more honest than most rough ML-for-physics manuscripts. You explicitly state that this is a single-generator-lineage validation study rather than a completed benchmark, and you repeatedly distinguish structural correctness from distributional correctness. That framing should stay. 

But **“Fast Monte Carlo Generator” is currently an evidence mismatch**. There is zero measured inference speed, zero Geant4 timing, and no complete end-to-end benchmark. The Discussion says exactly that.  The closest directly relevant ZDC flow-matching paper you cite actually reports concrete inference times, including 0.46 ms/sample and a latent-model result of 0.026 ms/sample, so a reader in this subfield will immediately expect timing from a title containing “Fast.” ([arXiv][1]) Until timing exists, I would regard “fast” as an intended use case, not an established property.

There is also a subtle contradiction between the Introduction saying the adverse results “localize model shortcomings” and the Discussion correctly saying that response/head/activity/support assignments are merely **hypotheses**, not established root causes.   “Help localize” would be scientifically safer.

“Reproducible data and geometry contract” is also stronger than what a reader can currently reproduce. The full geometry hash, configurations, validation reports, and data provenance live in an internal “project audit,” while the underlying dataset is not published.  At present this is **auditable internally**, not independently reproducible from the manuscript.

# Audit pass 2 — data lineage and leakage

This section needs a forensic audit before anything else.

The canonical split is 612,482/76,158/76,300, but the optimization lineage used a separate 26,624/6,656 pilot bank. The manuscript never states whether the pilot bank was constructed **from the canonical train/validation split after the canonical partition was frozen**, or whether it predates that partition. 

That distinction is enormous. If the pilot training events were selected before the canonical split, some of the 10,000 canonical-validation events used in this paper could theoretically have been pilot-training events. You need an explicit ID-level overlap assertion, ideally generated by code rather than prose.

The test history is worse. You disclose that 40,000 test events went through an external classifier and another 200 were used visually, with unresolved overlap.  This is admirably transparent, but the consequence is stronger than the current wording suggests. If you cannot reconstruct the identities of the 40,000 and 200 events, then you do **not know which individual members** of the remaining nominal test split are pristine. Knowing that between 36,100 and 36,300 must mathematically be untouched is not equivalent to knowing which 36,100–36,300 they are.

Also, “Neither earlier use informed model choices” is not a sufficiently strong statistical firewall. Once a test result has been observed, the set is no longer a conventionally untouched test set even if you believe no explicit model choice was based on it. The clean solutions are an identifiable untouched subset, newly generated Geant4 events, or a newly frozen holdout that has genuinely never been inspected.

# Audit pass 3 — is the conditioning actually sufficient?

Equation (2) says the target is \(p(Y\mid p^\mu,G)\), and the deterministic model features depend only on the four-vector. 

That is only the correct conditional distribution if **everything else capable of affecting the shower is either fixed or intentionally marginalized over with a fixed distribution**.

The biggest missing variable is the neutron trajectory geometry. Is the interaction/entry point fixed? Is the production vertex fixed? Does every neutron enter at the same transverse location? Are \(p_x,p_y\) actually nonzero over a meaningful angular range? If impact point varies event-to-event but is omitted from the condition, the generator is not learning the physical conditional distribution you claim; it is learning a mixture over unknown impact positions. That could directly explain spatial/topological discrepancies.

Likewise, if almost all events are pencil-beam \(p_x=p_y=0\), then describing the model as “four-momentum-conditioned” is formally true but scientifically much less significant than it sounds.

The paper needs an input-distribution figure or table: \(K_{\rm inc}\), \(\theta\), \(\phi\), entry position if applicable, number of unique/repeated conditions, and relevant correlations.

# Audit pass 4 — geometry semantics are not nailed down

The ganged-channel treatment deserves much more attention. You collapse several stable physical positions to an unweighted centroid. 

That is reasonable as one node coordinate for an ML model, but it becomes dangerous when you subsequently interpret **connected components on the frozen geometry graph** as spatial topology. 

If one readout channel physically represents multiple separated locations, its centroid can be somewhere no sensitive element actually exists. If adjacency is built from that centroid, graph connectivity can become an artifact. If adjacency instead represents the union of physical adjacencies of every ganged element, one node can artificially bridge disconnected physical regions.

You need to define exactly what “connected” means at the physical-position level versus the readout-channel level before treating component counts as physics.

The graph itself is underdescribed. “107,920 edges” is not enough. Are they directed? Within-layer only? Adjacent-layer? Euclidean k-NN? Radius graph? Are longitudinal edges included? How are the 400 ECAL nodes connected to HCAL layer 1? How are ganged channels handled? What are edge features?

# Audit pass 5 — the hierarchy is conceptually good, but mathematically underspecified

The factorization is understandable, but several states are not fully defined. 

If \(V=0\), what are \(L_0,A,D,K,H,r\)? Are they null-valued random variables, deterministic zeros, or simply absent? Right now the factorization formally continues sampling them. A clean generative definition needs a null branch.

There is also inconsistent notation between sampled variables carrying hats and others not carrying hats. \(L_0,A,H,r\) are just as sampled as \(\hat T,\hat D,\hat K\). And \(c=f_\phi(p^\mu)\) introduces \(\phi\), while the whole factorization is written as \(p_\theta\). Is \(\phi\subset\theta\), or are there two parameter sets?

More importantly, the flow representation of shares is underspecified. You say a flow “produces logits for nonnegative shares,” then softmaxes them.  Logits themselves are unconstrained real numbers; the shares become positive after softmax. What exactly is the training data transform \(x_1\)? Log shares? centered log-ratios? unconstrained logits? If it is a softmax-logit representation, how do you eliminate the additive gauge freedom \(r\mapsto r+\alpha\mathbf 1\)?

Variable-dimensional share flows also need explanation. A layer can have different \(K_\ell\). Are you using padded vectors plus a mask? One flow per \(K\)? A set model? What happens for \(K=1\), where the share is deterministically one and there is no stochastic simplex left to learn?

The same questions apply to layer-energy shares with variable active-layer masks.

# Audit pass 6 — “exact structural guarantees” need qualification

The exact-zero and energy-budget ideas are strong. Equation (4) does give exact zeros outside \(H_\ell\) and algebraic closure in real arithmetic. 

But the abstract says the decoder **guarantees finite nonnegative output**, which is stronger.

A neural ODE can generate NaNs or infinities. The original response branch involves an inverse log transform, which can overflow numerically. Softmax is positive in exact arithmetic, but in finite precision a sufficiently small exponent can underflow to exactly zero. In that case you can have \(|H_\ell|=K_\ell\) but fewer than \(K_\ell\) numerically positive energy cells.

So I need to know whether “hit count” means selected-support cardinality or actual `Y_i > 0` cardinality after floating-point decoding.

If you actually have runtime guards, stable log-sum-exp/softmax, finite-value assertions, clipping bounds, and a regression test demonstrating zero violations over millions of samples, then the claim can survive. Otherwise the mathematically precise claim is conditional: **given finite upstream quantities, the decoder algebraically enforces nonnegativity/support/closure**.

Also report the actual closure tolerance and observed worst-case violation. “Within floating-point tolerance” is not reproducible.

# Audit pass 7 — training versus inference mismatch may be a major hidden problem

The manuscript does not say what upstream variables downstream modules see during training.

When training the count head, does it receive **truth** \(D_\ell\) or generated \(\hat D_\ell\)? When training support, does it receive truth counts and layer energies or sampled ones? When training cell shares, does it receive truth support \(H\) or sampled support?

This matters enormously. If every downstream component is trained under oracle upstream variables and only exposed to generated upstream states at inference, you have classic hierarchical exposure error. The final model can have excellent isolated losses while joint generation collapses.

The sentence saying diagnostics proceed response → profile → count → support → share → joint and that later stages initialize from prior checkpoints does not resolve this. 

There is another subtle issue: the hard Gumbel-Top-\(k\) support decision is discrete. During “joint training,” how do losses downstream of support propagate into the support scorer? Straight-through estimator? Relaxation? No gradient at all? If the latter, “joint training” does not mean end-to-end joint optimization in the ordinary differentiable sense.

This needs an explicit training/inference dependency diagram.

# Audit pass 8 — the architecture-screen experiment is not yet causally interpretable

The manuscript correctly introduces M0-fresh because restarting Adam could itself change performance. 

But then Table 1 only shows **M0-fresh scalar loss**, while the dramatic S2 physical diagnostics are compared mainly with B0/V3-SUP.  

That is insufficient.

If the reason M0-fresh exists is to isolate optimizer-restart effects, you must show M0-fresh zero-response rate, C2ST, active-layer count, topology, response bias, etc. Otherwise you cannot know whether S2's 50.41% zeros came from the spline change, the restarted training dynamics, shared-encoder drift, or something else.

This is especially concerning because the stated intervention is the **positive-response head**, while the catastrophic failure appears in the separate **visibility hurdle**. If the encoder was jointly trainable, S2 is not a clean localized response-head intervention; the altered response loss can change shared features feeding the visibility head.

The stronger conclusion is currently: “the S2 training configuration changed the visibility behavior despite improving the selected objective,” not yet “replacing the positive response density caused this.”

And frankly, 50.41% zeros versus 0.93% truth is large enough that I would first investigate an implementation/checkpoint/label/shared-encoder problem before treating it as an interesting modeling tradeoff.

# Audit pass 9 — the loss itself needs a mathematical appendix

The fixed offset \(+0.421936354321\) deserves explicit derivation. 

If the historical model predicts density in

\(z=\log(1+T/s_T)\),

then conversion to a density with respect to \(T\) involves the Jacobian

\(dz/dT=1/(s_T+T)\).

Thus the NLL correction is event-dependent before averaging. A fixed offset can absolutely be valid **on one fixed dataset with one exact normalization convention**, but you need to show how that number was computed, which events it averages over, whether zeros are excluded, which loss coefficient multiplies it, and which base measure/units define the density.

This is particularly important because the full objective is not a true joint NLL anyway: it combines BCEs, CEs, ranking loss and flow-matching MSEs.  A lower scalar joint loss is therefore an optimization criterion, not a universal probabilistic statement that one model distribution is closer to truth.

S2 is basically demonstrating that point for you.

I would also want the nine individual component losses for M0-fresh and S2. If the visibility distribution goes from ~1% zeros to ~50% zeros while the total loss improves slightly, the component decomposition will probably tell you something important about the weighting.

# Audit pass 10 — C2ST needs a complete redesign/audit before Fig. 2 is trusted

This is my biggest concern.

The paper says every run uses the same 10,000-condition manifest and compares 10,000 generated events with 10,000 Geant4 validation events.  Then the condition-only classifier gets AUROC 0.4636. 

If each generated shower's condition is exactly copied from its matching Geant4 event, the two classes contain the same empirical condition multiset. A deterministic scoring function evaluated on both complete multisets therefore has identical positive- and negative-class score distributions and AUC 0.5.

A likely explanation is that you randomly split the **20,000 individual class examples** into classifier train/test sets instead of assigning each generated/Geant4 condition pair to the same fold. Then the classifier-test subsets no longer have exactly matched condition distributions. That can create spurious separability—or anti-separability—even though the original 10k/10k populations are condition matched.

If so, fix the evaluator by grouping on condition/event-pair ID when constructing folds.

The condition-only control is precisely the sanity check that should catch this. It currently appears to be catching something, but the manuscript dismisses it as “near chance.”

I would not publish Fig. 2 before resolving this.

The 0.65 threshold is also not physically interpretable. “Frozen” makes it procedurally clean, not scientifically meaningful. C2ST strength depends on classifier capacity, feature representation, sample size, training procedure, and regularization. A truth-vs-truth floor and permutation/null distribution are much more informative.

Finally, three **evaluator optimization seeds** are not statistical uncertainty on C2ST. You correctly say they are not generator-training seeds.  But the error bars therefore quantify classifier-training instability, not uncertainty in the model-vs-Geant4 discrepancy. You need data resampling/CV/permutation uncertainty separately.

# Audit pass 11 — one generated shower per condition cannot establish conditional fidelity

Your estimand is explicitly conditional. 

But if the 10,000-condition bank contains mostly unique four-vectors and you draw one generated shower at each, then most of the evaluation measures the joint mixture over conditions, not the full distribution at any fixed condition.

That is still useful, but it is weaker than the stated target.

A convincing conditional generator needs a repeated-condition battery: choose representative energies/directions/entry points, obtain many independent Geant4 showers and many model draws for each, then compare conditional means, variances, quantiles, occupancy distributions, correlations and multivariate distances.

You already mention repeated-condition coverage as planned.  I would move that much higher in the priority queue.

# Audit pass 13 — zero-threshold topology may be measuring numerical dust

You intentionally define the primary target at a zero-GeV threshold. That's coherent. 

But your strongest topology conclusions depend on whether a layer/channel is *exactly* nonzero. The model has 93.56% interior-gap events versus 52.43% for Geant4, and connected-component counts differ 58.99 versus 23.20. 

Before interpreting those as shower-physics structure, I would run a **diagnostic threshold sweep** without changing the declared primary target. For example: recompute the topology after suppressing deposits below several tiny energy thresholds.

If the discrepancy disappears after removing energetically negligible hits, then the zero-threshold topology mismatch is primarily a support-tail problem. If it persists, it is genuinely macroscopic shower structure. Either result is informative.

Connected-component counts should also be interpreted conditional on hit count. A model with the wrong occupancy can automatically have the wrong number of components even if its spatial organization conditional on \(K\) is reasonable.
