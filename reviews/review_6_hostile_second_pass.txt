# QA iteration 1 — your estimand is still not fully coherent

You write that the fixed generator vertex means entry position is not an independent condition, and the model conditions on the incident four-vector. 

That fixes one previous concern, but the argument is still incomplete.

A fixed generator vertex plus momentum uniquely determines a straight-line intersection with the calorimeter **only if the transport before the calorimeter does not introduce stochastic changes relevant to detector entry**. Yet several pages later you explicitly say upstream material is not documented. 

Suppose the primary neutron encounters upstream material. Then the detector-entry state can vary stochastically at fixed generator-level \(p^\mu\): position, direction, energy, multiplicity of secondaries, etc. In that case your simulator is not merely learning a calorimeter shower conditional on an entry four-vector. It is implicitly learning **upstream transport + calorimeter response conditional on the generator-level neutron**.

That is scientifically legitimate. But it is a different estimand.

I would explicitly distinguish:

\(p_{\mathrm{G4}}(Y\mid p^\mu_{\mathrm{gen}},\mathcal S)\)

from

\(p_{\mathrm{G4}}(Y\mid p^\mu_{\mathrm{entry}},x_{\mathrm{entry}},G)\).

Here \(\mathcal S\) is the complete fixed simulation configuration: detector geometry, material, upstream material, physics list, cuts, source vertex, etc.

Right now Eq. (3) pretending that \(G\) alone specifies the reference distribution is too narrow. Your own Section 3.4 proves that the reference law depends on much more than \(G\).

### Bigger problem: you never validate the angular conditioning

Your encoder explicitly uses \((u_x,u_y,u_z)\). 

Yet essentially every interpretable conditional result in the paper is stratified by **energy only**.

If the directions vary meaningfully, this is a huge hole. You are claiming a four-momentum-conditioned model while barely testing three of the four-dimensional condition degrees of freedom.

You need, eventually, at minimum:

response bias versus \(\theta\); response bias versus \(\phi\); zero rate versus angle; shower start versus angle; hit count versus angle; transverse centroid/width versus angle; and ideally 2D energy-angle maps.

If the angular spread is tiny, say so. If it is broad, this is one of the most important missing result families.

### Another ambiguity: 0–300 versus 50–250 GeV

The corpus spans 0–300 GeV, while Eq. (3) declares the scientific domain as 50–250 GeV. 

But I still cannot determine from the manuscript whether the 26,624 pilot-training events are sampled from 0–300 or only from 50–250.

That needs one explicit sentence. This matters for interpreting both training support and boundary behavior.

# QA iteration 2 — the ganged-channel problem is much bigger than the prose makes it sound

The introduction says “some HCAL readout IDs gang multiple physical locations.” 

Section 3.3 finally reveals that the number is **2,400 out of 6,390 HCAL channels**. 

That is **37.6% of all HCAL readout channels**.

“Some” badly understates this.

This may be one of the most consequential modeling decisions in the entire architecture.

A readout channel representing as many as four separated physical positions does not naturally have one Euclidean coordinate. Taking the centroid may put the graph node somewhere that contains no detector cell at all. Worse, neighborhoods around that centroid can be physically fictitious.

That affects at least three separate things:

The graph neural network sees potentially wrong geometry.

The connected-component metric sees potentially wrong topology.

Your high-level transverse centroids and radial RMS are computed from readout coordinates that may not correspond to the actual location where the deposited energy occurred.

The last point is particularly nasty. If a ganged readout aggregates two physically separated cells and only one fires, assigning all of \(Y_i\) to their centroid produces a geometrically false energy centroid.

Both Geant4 and generated events are treated identically in the readout representation, so it does not invalidate *distribution comparison in readout space*. But it absolutely weakens language that makes these quantities sound like physical shower-centroid measurements.

### Bigger architecture revision I would seriously consider

Represent each physical position as a **latent geometry node**, then tie multiple latent nodes to one output readout ID.

The model could message-pass on the true physical graph, produce latent spatial features, and aggregate to the ganged readout variable at the output.

Even if you do not generate latent energy separately, duplicated positional embeddings with a pooling operator would give the support network access to the true multimodal geometry.

At minimum, build a second graph where two readout IDs are adjacent if **any constituent physical positions are adjacent**, rather than connecting their centroids through kNN.

Your current sentence calling centroid distortion merely “a limitation of graph-based topology metrics” is too narrow. It is also potentially a limitation of the **model itself**.

# QA iteration 3 — I think there is a genuine mathematical issue in the flow coordinates

This one is subtle and important.

You define the active-layer flow target using a centered log share:

\(x_\ell=\log q_\ell-\frac1{|A|}\sum_{j\in A}\log q_j\),

modulo the \(10^{-8}\) clipping. 

Therefore, on the active coordinates,

\(\sum_{\ell\in A}x_\ell=0\).

So your data distribution lies on an \((|A|-1)\)-dimensional hyperplane.

The paper then says you interpolate from **masked Gaussian noise**.

If by that you mean independent \(N(0,1)\) entries on every active dimension, then the source distribution is full \(|A|\)-dimensional while the terminal data distribution lives on a codimension-one manifold.

A regular deterministic ODE flow cannot exactly transport a full-dimensional absolutely continuous distribution onto a lower-dimensional singular manifold without a degeneracy at the endpoint.

This disappears if your Gaussian is also centered:

\(\epsilon_\ell\sim N(0,1)\),

then

\(x_{0,\ell}=\epsilon_\ell-\frac1{|A|}\sum_{j\in A}\epsilon_j\).

Now both source and target live in the same zero-sum subspace.

**You need to check the implementation.**

If the Gaussian is already centered, say so explicitly. If it is not, I would change it.

The same issue applies to the cell-share flow.

For \(K_\ell=1\), it is even more obvious: there is only one possible share, namely 1, so its centered log coordinate is identically zero. Running a one-dimensional flow from Gaussian noise into a point mass is pointless. Just bypass the share flow when \(K=1\).

### The \(10^{-8}\) clipping also breaks exact invertibility

With

\(\tilde q_i=\max(q_i,10^{-8})\),

softmaxing the centered logs recovers

\(\tilde q_i/\sum_j\tilde q_j\),

not exactly the original \(q_i\).

That might be numerically negligible. Or it might matter for the long tail of tiny cell deposits.

You need to audit:

how many active layer shares are \(<10^{-8}\); how many cell shares are \(<10^{-8}\); total energy moved by the clipping; maximum event-level distortion.

Until then, “the analogous transform” hides a potentially meaningful preprocessing floor.

# QA iteration 4 — your response-hurdle factorization is internally inconsistent

This is another real mathematical inconsistency.

Section 4 defines \(V\) as nonzero deposited response and factorizes the model using \(p(V\mid c)p(\hat T\mid V,c)\). 

But Section 4.1 then says:

the Bernoulli hurdle can produce a visible event;

the Gaussian positive branch can sample \(z<0\);

you transform that into negative \(T\);

you clamp \(T\) to zero;

you then **reset \(V=0\)**. 

So the \(V\) in the factorization cannot simultaneously be the Bernoulli hurdle and final nonzero-response indicator.

Your actual process is something like:

\(H\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_H(c))\),

then if \(H=1\), sample \(z\),

then \(T=\max[s_T(\exp z-1),0]\),

then \(V=\mathbf 1[T>0]\).

Thus

\(P(V=0\mid c)\)

contains both hurdle failures **and** Gaussian-negative-tail mass.

That is not the stated factorization.

### The clean solution is much simpler

You already have a hurdle for the zero atom. Therefore the positive branch should have strictly positive support.

Instead of

\(z=\log(1+T/s_T)\),

use, for positive events,

\(z=\log(T/s_T)\),

and reconstruct

\(T=s_T e^z>0\).

Then the Bernoulli hurdle is the **only** mechanism that creates exact zeros.

That makes the probabilistic semantics dramatically cleaner.

A truncated Gaussian mixture on \(z>0\) would also work, but \(\log T\) is much simpler.

### Also: your “training-audit safety cap” is unacceptable without numbers

The paper says generated \(T\) is subject to a safety cap. 

What cap?

How was it chosen?

How many B0 samples hit it?

How many V3-SUP samples hit it?

Does hitting the cap create an atom at the cap?

Is a capped event included in evaluation?

If the hit count is zero, say that and the cap becomes a harmless runtime guard. If not, your generative law contains an undocumented tail modification.

What happens to a quarantined event?

Is it discarded?

Resampled?

Replaced by zeros?

Logged and still counted?

If you resample until success, you are sampling from the **model distribution conditioned on passing the checker**. That changes the probability law and affects timing.

If you drop failures, reported distributions are conditioned on acceptance.

# QA iteration 6 — your C2ST is still not clean enough to belong in the abstract

This is probably my biggest immediate problem with the current results.

You correctly discovered that the original random train/evaluation split broke matched condition pairs, and you fixed the condition-only control with pair-grouped splitting. But then you **retain all the substantive shower C2ST values from the old random-fold evaluator**. 

That is not enough.

The abstract's headline 0.779 is still produced by an evaluator whose fold construction you now know was wrong for paired data. 

You need to rerun **every C2ST family** with pair-grouped folds.

If you cannot rerun because event-level samples are gone, the 0.779 should be demoted from the abstract and presented explicitly as a historical exploratory diagnostic.

### Why pair splitting can matter even if the classifier does not receive the condition

Imagine one generated sample from condition \(c_i\) lands in classifier training and the corresponding Geant4 shower at the same condition lands in classifier test.

Because shower statistics encode incident energy and direction, the classifier can partially recognize the condition through \(Y\). Pair separation therefore creates dependencies between classifier train and test data.

The direction of bias need not even be predictable.

Your old condition-only AUC below 0.5 was a warning sign of exactly this pairing pathology.

### The new condition-only AUC of exactly 0.500 is not strong evidence

With pair-grouped splitting, every held-out condition occurs exactly once in each class.

Any deterministic function of condition alone therefore has **identical score multisets between the two classes**.

AUC 0.500 is essentially guaranteed.

That is a useful pipeline sanity check, but I would describe it as such rather than as evidence that the condition distribution is well controlled.

# QA iteration 7 — more seriously, your C2ST is not actually testing the conditional target very well

Your scientific estimand is

\(p(Y\mid p^\mu)\).

But the classifiers consume either high-level shower observables, the 6,790-channel vector, or the 65-dimensional layer profile. 

They apparently **do not receive \(p^\mu\)**.

That means they primarily test the marginal mixture

\(p(Y)=\int p(Y\mid c)p(c)\,dc\)

over your 10k development conditions.

That can miss conditional errors that cancel across conditions.

And your own Figure 3 gives a perfect example: response bias changes sign across energy bins. 

A generator could theoretically be too low at 150 GeV and too high at 190 GeV in such a way that the pooled \(Y\) distribution looks excellent.

### You should add conditional C2ST

Classify

\((c,Y)\)

rather than \(Y\) alone.

Because the condition distribution is exactly matched between generated and Geant4 classes, \(c\) itself carries no label information. But interactions between \(c\) and \(Y\) let the classifier detect incorrect conditional laws.

I would report both:

unconditional/marginal C2ST on \(Y\);

conditional C2ST on \((c,Y)\).

That would align the multivariate diagnostic with your actual scientific target.

# QA iteration 8 — the C2ST evaluator has more hidden details that need locking

You specify `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`, max depth, learning rate, iterations, and 70/30 split. Good improvement. 

But this is still not fully reproducible.

You need the scikit-learn version, `early_stopping`, `validation_fraction`, `min_samples_leaf`, `l2_regularization`, `max_bins`, and every nondefault option.

In particular, check `early_stopping`.

Depending on the sklearn version and sample count, histogram gradient boosting can create its **own internal validation split**. If you carefully group condition pairs in the outer split and then sklearn internally randomly partitions them again for early stopping, you may reintroduce the exact leakage you just fixed.

The safest audit configuration is probably:

pair-grouped outer folds;

`early_stopping=False`;

fixed `max_iter`;

and multiple group folds rather than three arbitrary 70/30 splits.

A cleaner evaluator would use five group-stratified condition folds, produce out-of-fold predictions for every event, then calculate one AUC and bootstrap **condition pairs** for its uncertainty.

That would be substantially more defensible than SD over three classifier seeds.

# QA iteration 12 — your support sampling scores are not probabilistically calibrated for Gumbel-Top-\(k\)

You now correctly disclose that class-balanced BCE + ranking is only a surrogate objective and not the Plackett–Luce likelihood induced by Gumbel-Top-\(k\). 

Good.

But there is a deeper consequence.

Gumbel-Top-\(k\) does not only use **rank order**. The scale of the logits relative to the unit-scale Gumbel noise determines how stochastic the selected set is.

Class-balanced BCE alters probability calibration.

A ranking loss does not identify a meaningful global score scale.

Therefore your score scale may be arbitrary for the sampling distribution.

A model with identical ranking but logits multiplied by ten gives much more deterministic support. Logits divided by ten give much more random support.

That is potentially consequential for your spatial fragmentation.

At minimum introduce a Gumbel temperature

\(s_i/\tau\)

and calibrate \(\tau\) on development data using a support-distribution diagnostic.

A more principled future version could train a set/list likelihood closer to the actual without-replacement sampling law.

Right now exact hit count is guaranteed, but **support stochasticity calibration is not**.

# QA iteration 13 — I suspect the support factorization itself can create spatial fragmentation

Your support stage selects exactly \(K_\ell\) channels in each layer through Gumbel-Top-\(k\). 

But I cannot tell whether sampled support in layer \(\ell\) is conditioned on the **actual sampled support in neighboring layers**.

The prose says “bidirectional layer context,” but that could simply mean deterministic neural context.

If every layer independently draws a support set conditional on condition, energy, count, and deterministic graph embeddings, then cross-layer stochastic alignment is weak.

That is almost designed to produce too many disconnected components.

A much stronger factorization would be something like

\(p(H)=\prod_\ell p(H_\ell\mid H_{<\ell},K,D,c,G)\).

Then the scorer for layer \(\ell\) can explicitly see which cells were selected in previous layers.

This is exactly the kind of inductive structure that L2LFlows and other layerwise methods exploit conceptually.

If your current support scorer already sees sampled neighboring support, say so. If not, I would elevate this as a major architectural candidate.

# QA iteration 29 — your common-measure derivation is much better, but still mathematically sloppy about units

This revision fixed one of my previous concerns very well: you now show that the 0.421936 correction is the average log-Jacobian times the response-loss weight and explicitly say it is not universal. 

Good.

One subtlety remains.

You write

$$
\log(s_T+T).
$$

But \(s_T+T\) has units of GeV.

Strictly, taking the logarithm of a dimensional quantity is undefined.

What you are actually doing numerically is almost certainly something like

\(t=T/(1\,\mathrm{GeV})\),

\(s=10\),

then

$$
-\log p_t(t)
=
-\log p_z(z)+\log(s+t).
$$

That also makes explicit that continuous-density NLL depends on the chosen reference measure/unit up to an additive constant.

Since this entire section is explicitly about putting two heads onto the same density measure, being rigorous here is worth it.

# The next revision sequence I would use

1. **Rerun every C2ST with condition-pair-grouped folds.** Do not leave the 0.779 headline dependent on the known-bad random split.

2. **Add conditional C2ST using \((p^\mu,Y)\)** as well as marginal C2ST using \(Y\) alone.

3. **Audit sklearn's internal early stopping and every evaluator parameter**, and use condition-group cross-validation plus condition-pair bootstrap uncertainty.

4. **Audit S2 before treating it as a scientific result:** visibility logits, BCE contribution, state-dict loading, optimizer membership, probability convention, config inheritance, and visible-only C2ST.

5. **Replace the positive response transform** with one having strictly positive support, ideally \(T=s_Te^z\), so the hurdle is the sole zero atom. Until then, distinguish the hurdle variable from final visibility in the factorization.

6. **Check the flow base distribution.** If Gaussian noise is not centered on the zero-sum subspace, fix it. Audit the \(10^{-8}\) clipping frequency and energy distortion.

7. **Specify quarantine and safety-cap behavior quantitatively.** Report cap value, cap frequency, numerical rejection count, and whether rejected samples are dropped or resampled.

8. **Clarify the training distribution:** exactly how the 26,624 pilot events were selected, whether they span 0–300 or 50–250 GeV, and their energy/angular distributions.

9. **Add angle-dependent validation.** Right now the manuscript barely tests why \(p_x,p_y,p_z\) are in the model.

10. **Treat ganged geometry as a major modeling problem, not a footnote.** Quantify 37.6% explicitly and build an independent/physical adjacency representation. Strongly consider latent physical-position nodes.

11. **Run threshold-stability tests** for activity gaps and connected components before calling them physical fragmentation.

12. **Add stagewise diagnostics** for visibility, total response, \(L_0\), activity sequence, layer budgets, hit counts, support geometry, and cell shares. Your architecture practically begs for this.

13. **Generate multiple sampling seeds from existing checkpoints now.** This is a cheap way to quantify generator-sampling variability before expensive multi-training-seed runs.

14. **Run at least a training-scale curve** before interpreting the failure mechanistically. A single model trained on 4.35% of the available training data cannot support an architecture-level negative conclusion.

15. **Only after the science stabilizes, clean the manuscript voice:** remove project-management language, arbitrary internal gates from figures, defensive wording, submission TODOs, and tiny figure fonts.

The big change in my assessment from the previous audit is that **the paper's remaining weaknesses are no longer mainly missing explanations**. Several are now genuine experimental or mathematical questions exposed by the improved documentation: the CLR-flow source manifold, the mismatch between the stated hurdle factorization and clamp/reset sampling, the uncalibrated score scale entering Gumbel-Top-\(k\), conditional versus marginal C2ST, the condition-pair fold problem, and the enormous ganged-geometry approximation.

Those are the places I would attack before adding another paragraph of polish.
