Short version: **the draft is scientifically self-aware, but right now it reads much more like an unusually rigorous internal validation report than a submission-ready “fast Monte Carlo generator” paper.** The biggest problems are not grammar. They are experimental identifiability, incomplete method specification, unclear publication thesis, a compromised/ambiguous test-set history, and several places where the evidence shown does not actually support the causal interpretation implied by the prose.

The strongest thing you are doing is explicitly refusing to equate structural correctness with physics fidelity: you say the decoder closes exactly while the generated distribution is still wrong, and you report adverse validation results rather than hiding them.  That is good science. But it also creates the central problem: **what is this paper claiming to contribute today?** If the answer is “a validated fast simulator,” the data do not support it. If the answer is “a hierarchical sparse architecture plus a rigorous failure analysis,” that can potentially be defensible, but the paper needs to be reorganized around that thesis.

## Pass 1 — What paper is this actually trying to be?

The title says **“A Hierarchical Sparse Fast Monte Carlo Generator”**, while the abstract explicitly says physics fidelity and end-to-end speedup have *not* been established.   That mismatch will be the first thing a hostile reviewer notices.

Right now I see at least four possible papers competing with each other: a new generative architecture paper; a detector-specific fast-simulation paper; a negative-result/validation paper; or a reproducibility/governance methodology paper. You have pieces of all four and therefore do not yet have one dominant scientific story.

The sentence that the current evidence is “a single-generator-lineage validation study, not a completed multi-seed benchmark” is admirably precise, but it is also basically an admission that the paper is an interim report.  Similarly, “The potential publication outcome may be negative” reads like a lab notebook/project plan rather than the Discussion of a finished paper. 

My current judgment: **do not spend much effort polishing prose yet. First decide what claim survives even if the final model remains bad.**

## Pass 2 — The target distribution is still underspecified

You define the target as deposited readout energy conditional on the neutron four-vector and fixed geometry, with \(50\le K_{\rm inc}\le250\) GeV.  Mathematically that is clean. Physically, however, too much surrounding information is missing.

The manuscript never tells me whether incident position is fixed, whether angle is fixed or variable, the ranges of \(p_x,p_y,p_z\), whether the neutron enters at the detector center, whether the source point varies, whether all particles cross the same front face, or whether the corpus contains arbitrary incidence. Those details determine whether conditioning on the four-vector is sufficient.

There is another terminology problem: you alternate among “visible response,” “deposited response,” “deposited readout energy,” and “raw deposited energy.” Those are not automatically interchangeable detector concepts. If \(Y_i\) is literally Geant4 energy deposited in sensitive material, call it that. If scintillation, sampling, SiPM response, quenching, digitization, or electronics enter, define them. The current wording risks implying detector-level response when the target may be an energy-deposition truth quantity.

The phrase “stored non-sentinel deposited hit energy” also hides a potentially critical preprocessing operation.  What is a sentinel? Why does it exist? Can stored values be negative? Was there already zero suppression in the production file? A topology metric based on “inactive” layers is extremely sensitive to hidden storage thresholds.

## Pass 3 — The detector/geometry description is not remotely sufficient yet

You give 400 ECAL channels, 6,390 HCAL channels, 65 layers, a 90-channel final HCAL layer, and a 107,920-edge graph.   That tells me the tensor dimensions. It does not tell me enough to understand the detector.

A serious calorimeter paper needs, at minimum, the physical segmentation, material sequence, dimensions, what “layer” means, where layer 0 sits, whether the 400 ECAL channels form one longitudinal layer, whether the remaining 64 layers are HCAL, how adjacency edges are constructed, whether edges are directed, whether self-loops exist, whether edges connect adjacent longitudinal layers, and how graph distances correspond to physical distances.

The ganged-channel treatment deserves special scrutiny. You represent a ganged readout channel by the unweighted centroid of several stable physical positions.  If those physical positions are not spatially contiguous, the centroid may be a location at which no detector element exists. That could distort both graph message passing **and your connected-component validation metric**. This may be harmless if the ganged positions are local; it may be a serious geometry artifact if they are separated. The paper currently gives me no way to know.

## Pass 4 — The mathematical factorization is elegant but hides important implementation semantics

Equation (3) is one of the strongest parts conceptually: visibility → total → shower start/activity → layer budgets → counts → support → shares.  But the manuscript skips exactly the implementation details that determine whether this factorization works.

For example, what happens to \(L_0,A,D,K,H,r\) when \(V=0\)? Are they never sampled, sampled and discarded, or deterministically assigned null values? What happens if the activity head selects an active layer with effectively zero layer budget? Does the count head get truth \(D_\ell\) or predicted \(D_\ell\) during training? Does the support head train on truth count/support and then receive sampled counts at inference? Does the share flow train on truth support and infer on generated support? If yes, there is teacher-forcing/exposure-shift throughout the hierarchy, which could plausibly explain the joint failure even when isolated components look acceptable.

The statement that the profile and cell-share flows use conditional flow matching gives only the generic straight interpolation.  You have not defined the actual data transformations into Euclidean space. That is especially important because layer and cell shares live on simplices. “For a data transform \(x_1\)” is doing a huge amount of work. What transform? Additive log-ratio? centered log-ratio? logits against a reference? \(\log q\) with normalization? epsilon regularization? How are very small shares handled?

You also do not specify the ODE solver, number of integration steps, tolerances, discretization, batching, or whether cell-share flows are integrated separately for each active layer. Those details matter simultaneously for fidelity **and the word “fast.”**

## Pass 5 — Your “exact structural guarantees” need a qualification

The decoder guarantees exact support cardinality and energy-budget closure *given finite valid upstream outputs*. Equation (4) makes that clear.  But the abstract says the decoder “guarantees finite nonnegative output.” 

A neural network/ODE can emit NaNs, infinities, or logits large enough to cause numerical failure unless you use a stable softmax/log-sum-exp implementation and enforce finite-state checks. So I would distinguish a **mathematical decoder invariant conditional on finite inputs** from an **empirically checked runtime invariant**. Otherwise “guarantees finite” is slightly too strong.

Also, “a single Gumbel-Top-\(k\) draw selects the channels” in the abstract sounds like one global draw. The method says one draw **for each active layer**.  Fix that ambiguity.

There is a deeper modeling question here. Gumbel-Top-\(k\) gives weighted sampling without replacement, corresponding to a Plackett-Luce-type mechanism. ([Proceedings of Machine Learning Research][1]) Your support model is trained using support BCE plus a ranking loss, but generated using this coupled without-replacement sampler. Is the training objective actually matched to the induced subset distribution, or are the learned logits merely ranking scores? That mismatch deserves explicit thought.

## Pass 6 — The training protocol is drastically underreported

You describe nine loss terms and staged initialization, but not the actual architecture and optimization recipe.  Missing are parameter counts, hidden dimensions, number of graph layers, activation functions, normalization, optimizer hyperparameters, learning-rate schedule, batch size, epochs, early stopping, gradient clipping, numerical precision, training hardware, training wall time, data-loader behavior, augmentation if any, and checkpoint-selection rule.

This is not optional detail for a generative-model paper.

The pilot-data issue is particularly important. The canonical training split has 612,482 events, but the reported optimization lineage used only 26,624 training events.  That is about **23× fewer training examples than the canonical training set**. If the present model is badly mismatched to Geant4, the immediate reviewer question is going to be: *is this architecture failing, or is this a deliberately undertrained pilot?*

You need a scientific rationale for why results from a ~26k-event pilot are worth interpreting beyond software validation.

## Pass 7 — The test-set history is your most serious governance problem

You state that 40,000 test events were used in an external classifier study and 200 test events in a visual draw, with unresolved overlap, leaving somewhere between 36,100 and 36,300 never used. 

That is candid, but “neither earlier use informed model choices” does not magically make the original 76,300-event test set pristine again.

The cleanest solution is probably to **burn the exposed portion permanently** and formally define a new untouched final subset before any more development. But that only works if you can identify which events were used. If the exact 40k and 200 event IDs cannot be reconstructed, you have a more serious problem.

Do not wait until the final paper to resolve this. Resolve it now.

## Pass 8 — The validation battery has good instincts but incomplete statistical definition

Your fixed 10,000-condition bank and explicit separation of generator-training seeds from evaluator seeds is excellent.  But the C2ST is underspecified.

What classifier architecture? What train/validation/test split? Is reported AUROC held out? How are generated and truth samples assigned to folds? Are matched conditions kept together? Are condition variables themselves included in high-/low-/profile-aware feature families? What are the exact feature lists? What preprocessing occurs? Are evaluator hyperparameters frozen? What prevents the classifier from learning bookkeeping artifacts rather than shower physics?

The condition-only AUROC of 0.4636 deserves explanation.  If generated and Geant4 events use exactly the same 10,000-condition manifest, their condition distribution is not merely similar; it should be paired-identical. A condition-only classifier should therefore asymptotically be at chance. 0.4636 is plausibly finite-sample variation, but I want to see precisely how classifier folds are formed.

The 0.65 “frozen diagnostic threshold” also needs a rationale.  Why 0.65? Was it chosen empirically, before seeing B0, from a calibration study, or just as an engineering gate? If it is an internal QA threshold, say that explicitly rather than letting it resemble a statistically meaningful acceptance criterion.

And three evaluator seeds are not a substitute for uncertainty quantification. Bootstrap confidence intervals over events/conditions would be more informative.

## Pass 9 — The S2 causal interpretation is not yet supported by the figures you show

This is one of the biggest logical problems in the paper.

Section 5.2 correctly says every screen must be compared against **M0-fresh**, because optimizer restart itself has a measurable cost.  Great.

But your key S2 diagnostic figures compare S2 against **B0 and/or V3-SUP**, not M0-fresh. Figure 2 shows B0, V3-SUP, S2.  Figure 6 shows Geant4 vs S2 and Geant4/B0/V3-SUP, again without M0-fresh. 

So what is the M0-fresh zero-response rate? What is its C2ST AUROC? What is its active-layer count? Without those numbers, you have not isolated how much of S2's collapse is attributable to the spline architecture versus fresh-optimizer retraining.

This matters even more because “alter one mechanism at a time” does **not** mean only that mechanism's output will move if joint training unfreezes the shared encoder/full network. A response-head architecture change can alter gradients into the shared encoder, which then moves the visibility head. That may be exactly what happened. But it needs to be demonstrated rather than rhetorically assigned to the response spline.

\(4.921213-4.935508=-0.014295\), not \(-0.014296\).

Also, “Recorded decision” feels like experiment-tracking metadata rather than manuscript material. It may belong in an appendix/evidence ledger unless screening decisions are themselves part of the paper's methodological contribution.

## Pass 11 — The response metrics need exact equations

Section 6.2 is conceptually right that pooled response statistics can hide conditional failure.  But I cannot reproduce several reported metrics from the manuscript.

“Mean positive-response bias”: is it \((\mu_{\rm gen}-\mu_{\rm G4})/\mu_{\rm G4}\)? Conditioned separately on \(T>0\) in each sample? Conditioned on truth being positive? What happens to generated zero events?

“Response-resolution difference”: what is resolution? \(\sigma/\mu\)? RMS/mean? standard deviation? 68% interval? And is the plotted number an absolute difference or relative percentage difference?

“Paired generated-minus-truth residual”: you correctly warn that it is not reconstruction error, but why use a paired event-level RMS at all when the generated and Geant4 showers are independent stochastic realizations at the same condition? That RMS folds together intrinsic Geant4 variance and model variance.

“Relative \(L^1\) difference 0.1098 in the battery’s definition” is not enough.  Put the equation in the paper.

No response plot has uncertainty intervals. With eight bins, I want bootstrap error bars or confidence bands before deciding which bin-to-bin oscillations are meaningful.

## Pass 12 — The longitudinal/topology result may be your best scientific finding, but it needs much stronger definition

The paper's clearest failure mode is that active-layer count is nearly right while arrangement is badly wrong: 53.53 vs 54.08 active layers, but 93.56% vs 52.43% interior-gap fraction and ~59 vs 23 connected components. 

That is genuinely informative. But I need to know whether it is robust to the exact definition of “active.”

At a nominal 0-GeV threshold, any nonzero stored energy activates a layer. But Geant4 files often have production/storage semantics that mean “zero threshold” in downstream analysis is not literally infinite detector sensitivity. If tiny hits are stored differently between truth and generator, gap statistics can become pathological.

Connected-component count also needs conditioning. If generated showers have different hit multiplicity, 59 vs 23 components may partially be a multiplicity effect. Show component count versus number of hit cells, or at least report multiplicity jointly.

And because your graph definition is central, this metric is only meaningful if graph edges correspond cleanly to physical adjacency—bringing us back to the ganged-channel centroid issue.

## Pass 13 — The zero-response S2 failure is dramatic, but the loss decomposition is missing

The S2 result is striking: 50.41% generated zeros versus 0.93% truth, while selected loss improves slightly.  This is probably the strongest pedagogical result in the draft.

But I want the nine-term loss decomposition for M0-fresh and S2. The joint loss drops by only about 0.014 on a ~4.94 total, while a major physical marginal catastrophically deteriorates. Which component improves enough to compensate? Did the visibility BCE itself worsen? Did response NLL dominate the scale? Are loss weights miscalibrated? Did shared-encoder gradient interference move the visibility head?

Without that decomposition, “aggregate objective and physical targets must be read together” is true but shallow. With the decomposition, you could make a concrete optimization diagnosis.

## Pass 14 — The architecture-screening program is internally incomplete

You list S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S7, D1, and D2, then only report completed rows for S1–S3. 

Why no S6? Maybe intentional naming history, but reviewers will wonder.

More importantly, if several are implemented but unevaluated, do they belong in the manuscript at all? Naming future experimental variants makes the document feel like a progress memo. Either make this an explicit preregistered experimental program or remove future branches until results exist.

The full-size critic having a 24-GiB memory blocker is similarly implementation-log detail unless it motivates a scientifically relevant computational constraint.

At present the novelty boundary is blurry.

## Pass 19 — You need much more Geant4 provenance

The manuscript tells me that Geant4 is the reference but not enough about the simulation that generated those 764,940 events. 

I need Geant4 version, physics list, detector-geometry version, production cuts, step limits if relevant, sensitive-volume definition, whether optical photons are simulated, neutron source distribution, energy distribution, angular distribution, entry position, random-seed policy, and whether all events come from one production campaign.

## Pass 20 — The conclusion is appropriately cautious but currently undersells what a finished negative-result paper would need

The conclusion says generated and reference showers remain distinguishable and that a final performance claim requires multi-seed testing, governed test evaluation, baselines, reconstruction, and timing. 

That is correct.

But if you eventually publish a *negative result*, the bar is not merely “the model failed.” You need to show that the failure survived credible training scale, hyperparameter effort, multiple seeds, fair baselines, and diagnostic ablations. Otherwise the obvious interpretation is “this pilot implementation was not yet optimized.”
