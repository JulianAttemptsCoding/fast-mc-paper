You define the scientific target as

# Pass 4 — Your generative factorization is not actually consistent with the implementation

This is subtle and important.

You define \(V\) as **nonzero deposited response** and factorize the model beginning with

\(p(V\mid c)\,p(\hat T\mid V,c)\). 

But Section 4.1 then says that when the Bernoulli branch says positive response, the Gaussian mixture can produce \(z<0\); you transform it, clamp the resulting negative \(T\) to zero, and **reset \(V=0\)**. 

Those are not the same random variable anymore.

You effectively have:

1. a hurdle branch indicator;
2. a positive-branch continuous draw;
3. a clamp-induced second zero mechanism;
4. a final realized indicator \(1[\hat T>0]\).

Your current graphical/factorized story conflates 1 and 4.

### Fix the notation

Call the first Bernoulli variable something like \(B\), not \(V\):

\(B\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\pi(c))\).

Then define:

\(B=0\Rightarrow\hat T=0\).

When \(B=1\), sample \(z\), transform it, and define the resulting \(\hat T\).

Then if you want a final visibility variable,

\(V=\mathbf 1[\hat T>0]\).

You can then write the zero mass explicitly:

\(P(\hat T=0\mid c)=P(B=0\mid c)+P(B=1\mid c)P(z\le0\mid B=1,c)\),

ignoring any other numerical rejection mechanism.

That would make the response-model pathology crystal clear.
