This is **substantially more defensible than the prior version**, but the better documentation is now exposing the real scientific weaknesses instead of hiding them.

The manuscript is currently a **good development/audit document and an incomplete scientific paper**. I would not submit this version. The problem is no longer that the manuscript is vague about what it did; the problem is that several things it now clearly says it did are scientifically weak, unfinished, or internally awkward.

channel count;

# Audit iteration 8: your training density is not the probability law you actually sample

This is important.

The positive-response loss models

\(z=\log(1+T/10\,\mathrm{GeV})\)

with a Gaussian mixture and uses the Jacobian-corrected density. 

But inference then applies:

negative-tail clamping;

the deterministic \(C(K)\) cap;

and the Bernoulli hurdle.

Therefore the density whose NLL you optimize is **not the density of the final \(T\) produced by the generator**.

The generated law has:

an atom at zero;

a continuous interior;

potentially an atom or pile-up at \(C(K)\) if clipping activates.

Your Eq. (7) only describes the uncapped continuous positive branch.

### Revision

Say this explicitly.

Something like:

> The response NLL is a surrogate objective for the latent positive branch rather than a normalized likelihood of the complete postprocessed response law.

That distinction becomes especially relevant because your paper contains a section about **scalar objective versus generation behavior**.

# Audit iteration 9: Eq. (7) has slightly misleading probability notation

You write

\(-\log p_t(t\mid c)\). 

But the total response distribution conditional on \(c\) contains a discrete zero atom.

So \(p_t(t\mid c)\) cannot globally mean an ordinary continuous density over the whole response variable.

What you actually mean is something like

\(p_t(t\mid V_{\rm ref}=1,c)\)

or

\(p_t^+(t\mid c)\).

Use notation that makes the hurdle structure explicit.

That will prevent mathematically literate readers from objecting to mixing a density and a mass function under one symbol.

# Audit iteration 10: the positive-branch zero atom should eventually be removed, not merely documented

You now clearly distinguish \(B\) from realized \(V\), which fixes a major semantic problem from the earlier draft. 

But the design is still unnecessarily ugly.

You already have a Bernoulli branch whose entire purpose is to model exact zero response.

Then the “positive” branch also has probability mass mapping to zero because a Gaussian can produce \(z<0\).

You report that the extra atom happened to produce zero observed events in Dbat, so it is not currently driving the discrepancy. 

Still: why keep it?

For a future architecture, model positive response as

\(z=\log(T/s)\)

and decode

\(T=s\exp z>0\).

Then there is exactly one zero mechanism.

Cleaner generative semantics.

Cleaner likelihood.

Cleaner debugging.

Cleaner paper.

# Audit iteration 11: the activity model almost invites the failure you observe

This may be the central architectural criticism.

You explicitly state:

> subsequent layer activity is modeled as conditionally independent Bernoulli variables given \((c,T,L_{\rm first})\).



Then you find:

mean active layers: 54.08 truth versus 53.53 model — within about **1%**;

interior-gap events: 52.43% versus **93.56%**;

mean gaps: 2.10 versus **6.25**. 

This is almost a textbook failure of matching marginals while missing dependence.

The model gets roughly the right number of active bits but places them with the wrong joint structure.

That is much more interesting than merely saying “there are too many gaps.”

### What you should measure

Adjacent-layer transition probabilities.

\(P(A_{\ell+1}=1\mid A_\ell=1)\).

\(P(A_{\ell+1}=0\mid A_\ell=1)\).

Binary autocorrelation versus lag.

Number of active runs.

Longest active run.

Longest inactive interior run.

Gap-length distribution.

Shower termination probability versus depth.

Start-to-last-active span.

These would directly test the dependence structure the independent Bernoulli model cannot express.

### Architecture revision

An autoregressive binary sequence over 65 layers is tiny.

There is almost no reason not to use

\(p(A\mid\cdots)=\prod_\ell p(A_\ell\mid A_{<\ell},c,T,L_0)\).

Do not force contiguity—Geant4 itself has gaps—but **learn dependence**.

# Audit iteration 12: your CLR flow is mathematically acknowledged, but still unnecessarily malformed

The manuscript now openly states that independent Gaussian source noise contains an additive CLR-gauge component while the target sums to zero, and that terminal softmax removes the gauge. 

That transparency is good.

But the design is still strange.

Your target lives in

\(\{x:\sum_i x_i=0\}\),

while the Gaussian source does not.

So one component of the learned velocity is dedicated to contracting an irrelevant direction that softmax later discards anyway.

### Better options

Center the Gaussian source:

\(x_0=\epsilon-\bar\epsilon\mathbf1\).

Or project every vector-field output onto the zero-sum subspace.

Or use an ILR basis with exactly \(d-1\) independent coordinates.

Any of those makes the geometry of source and target match.

### Also audit \(K=1\)

For a one-channel layer, the share is deterministically 1.

The CLR is identically zero.

Do not integrate an ODE for that case.

Just return share \(1\).

# Audit iteration 18: your Gumbel-Top-\(k\) score scale remains unidentified

You now correctly say support BCE + ranking is a surrogate and not the likelihood of the exact-size Gumbel sampler. 

There is a deeper issue:

Gumbel-Top-\(k\) stochasticity depends on the **scale of the scores relative to the Gumbel noise**.

A ranking loss only cares about order.

Class-balanced BCE induces some scale, but that scale is distorted by class weighting.

So even if rank quality is excellent, support diversity may be much too deterministic or much too random.

This could directly contribute to topology problems.

### Minimal revision

Introduce a support temperature \(\tau\):

\(s_i\rightarrow s_i/\tau\).

Calibrate \(\tau\) on development data using support-level diagnostics.

### Diagnostics

Pairwise support inclusion frequencies.

Per-channel occupancy calibration.

Jaccard distribution between independent model draws.

Nearest-neighbor spacing.

Radial occupancy.

Connected-component distribution conditional on \(K\).

Support entropy/diversity at fixed condition.
