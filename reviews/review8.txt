# Audit pass 2 — the response sampler has **three** support components, not the two you describe

This is probably the most important mathematical catch in this draft.

You correctly fixed the lower-support issue. The implementation now has:

1. a Bernoulli zero atom through \(B=0\);
2. another zero atom when the Gaussian mixture gives \(z_s\le0\), because negative \(T_{\rm raw}\) is clamped to zero. 

But you also define

\(\tilde T=\min\{T_{\rm raw},C(K_{\rm inc})\}.\)

That creates **another discrete atom at the upper cap**.

Because your Gaussian mixture has unbounded support, for every finite \(C(K)\),

\(P(T_{\rm raw}\ge C(K)\mid c)>0\)

mathematically. Therefore the actual sampler has

\(P(T=C(K)\mid B=1,c)>0.\)

The manuscript never states this.

You therefore have a mixed distribution consisting of:

* mass at zero;
* continuous density on \(0<T<C(K)\);
* mass at \(T=C(K)\).

But Eq. (7) gives the ordinary continuous change-of-variable density for the **uncapped** positive Gaussian-mixture transform. 

### Why this matters

If the cap activates with non-negligible probability, the response NLL you train is **not the likelihood of the distribution you actually sample**.

This is another training/inference mismatch on top of teacher forcing.

Even if the cap activation probability is microscopic, the mathematical model specification should say so.

### Immediate revision

Add something like:

> “The deterministic upper clipping also induces a point mass at \(C(K_{\rm inc})\). Equation (7) describes the uncapped continuous response objective rather than the complete capped sampling law.”

Then measure it.

If cap activation is literally zero in all relevant generated samples, say that and make it a regression counter.

Better still, consider replacing the clipping rule with either a properly normalized bounded/truncated distribution or a safety check that **flags** an excursion rather than silently changing the sample.

# Audit pass 10 — the activity model is not merely imperfect; it is structurally unable to represent residual activity correlations

You now make the architecture explicit:

> “Subsequent layer activity is modeled as conditionally independent Bernoulli variables given \((c,T,L_{\rm first})\).”

For two distinct downstream layers \(i,j\), your model imposes, conditional on those variables,

\(\operatorname{Cov}(A_i,A_j\mid c,T,L_{\rm first})=0.\)

Geant4 is under no such constraint.

Construct a baseline using **reference marginal per-layer activity probabilities but force layers to be independent**.

1. estimate \(P_{\rm G4}(A_\ell=1\mid\) coarse energy/first-layer condition\()\);

3. compute interior-gap fraction.

That would be an excellent result.

Right now the strongest defensible answer is:

> **A carefully constrained hierarchical generator can satisfy exact output semantics while still producing substantially wrong joint shower structure, and this particular development lineage exposes several mechanisms by which that can happen.**

That is a solid development conclusion.

It is **not yet**:

> this architecture fundamentally fails,

and it is definitely not yet:

> this is a validated fast Monte Carlo surrogate.

The next version should be driven less by prose polishing and more by four decisive pieces of evidence: **finish/check convergence of V3-SUP; audit the response cap; run the oracle cascade; and replace/test the independent-Bernoulli activity model.** Those four could materially change the scientific story.
