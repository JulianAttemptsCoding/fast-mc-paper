## 1. Blocking issues

### B1. The component excess mostly does *not* come from longitudinal gaps

This contradicts the abstract and §6.1. Using only your definitions and Table 1:

```
Per nonempty event:  m ≥ R  (your §4)   and   R − 1 ≤ G  (each run boundary needs ≥1 empty layer)

Generator:  E[m − R] ≥ 59.39 − (1 + 6.05) = 52.34
Reference:  E[m − R] ≤ 23.42 − 1         = 22.42
⇒ Δ E[m − R] ≥ 29.92  of the +35.97  (≥ 83%)
⇒ Δ E[R]     ≤ 6.05                  (≤ 17%)
```

- §6.1 says the aggregates "do not establish an independent lateral-fragmentation effect." That is false; they do.
- The abstract sentence saying the component count "cannot be interpreted independently of the gaps" must go.
- The title and framing are inverted. The dominant structural failure is fragmentation *within* occupied runs.
- Call it "within-run" rather than "lateral." A break inside a run can also come from the 4-NN longitudinal edges failing to link adjacent occupied layers.
- The largest-component fraction points the same way. 11.1% of 1588.8 is about 177 channels spread over about 58 other components, roughly 3 channels each. These are many small islands.

### B2. The gap discrepancy follows directly from Eq. (4)

- **What the model assumes:** A_ℓ is conditionally independent given (h_c, T, F, ℓ), and there is no event latent (§3).
- **What that predicts:** Independent Bernoullis with correct marginals reproduce E[#active] = Σp_ℓ, which is your +0.25. When real activity is positively dependent (showers end early or late as a whole), independence pushes the last active layer outward and punches interior holes. That matches your +4.42 last-layer shift and the 93.5% gap fraction.
- **The physics:** Longitudinal fluctuations in hadronic showers are driven mainly by the depth of the first inelastic interaction. No variable in your model carries that depth.
  - F cannot carry it. At zero threshold, F records the first deposit of *any* kind (soft scatters, backsplash into the ECAL), not where the cascade starts.
  - T correlates with depth only weakly.
- **Why the oracle cascade won't settle it:** The test proposed in §6.1 will not isolate the cause. Teacher-forcing T and F still leaves Eq. (4) independent.
- **Decisive test:** This needs no checkpoint, only reference events.

```python
# reference nonempty showers: A (n×65 bool), T, F, Kinc
# 1. bin by (Kinc bin, T quantile, F); p[bin, l] = A[bin, l].mean() for l > F
# 2. A_null[e, l] ~ Bernoulli(p[bin(e), l]) for l > F;  A_null[e, F] = 1;  A_null[e, l<F] = 0
# 3. compute L, span, G, gap fraction on A_null; compare to Table 1 generator column
```

- **What a positive result gives you:** If the null reproduces roughly +4 layers and roughly 90% gap fraction, "does not identify the cause" becomes a result.
- **Fix class:** make activity Markov or autoregressive in ℓ, or add an explicit shower-start or shower-end latent. This is the motivation behind layer-to-layer designs: L2LFlows trains one flow per calorimeter layer, conditioned on the preceding five layers, specifically to learn layer-to-layer correlations.

### B3. Zero threshold with no time window is not a readout observable

- The reference averages 1617.6 of 6,790 channels active and 54.6 of 65 layers active per shower.
- At zero threshold, these counts are dominated by sub-MIP deposits: elastic neutron scatters, capture gammas and late hits. Their number depends on exactly the metadata you lack: physics list (including high-precision neutron transport), production cuts, and a time window, which is never mentioned.
- Your gap, last-layer and component observables are therefore the most metadata-sensitive quantities in the paper.
- Your intro's premise ("readout properties relevant to detector studies") requires a physics threshold and an integration window.
- **Fix:** make the primary analysis use a threshold appropriate to each technology, for example 0.5 MIP-equivalent in the tiles, plus a realistic time cut. Keep zero threshold as a sensitivity row. The result could change qualitatively in either direction.
- **Missing plot:** the most diagnostic figure for this is absent. Show the per-hit energy spectrum (log x-axis) for generator vs reference, split by ECAL/HCAL and by depth.

### B4. "1.0% mean total deposit" is not a statement about detector response

- I believe the ECAL is a homogeneous crystal and the HCAL a steel/scintillator sampling stack (see B5). Summing raw deposits across them adds quantities with very different visible fractions.
- You give no sampling fraction. For a steel/scintillator stack it is typically a few percent. In that case:
  - −0.129 GeV visible in the HCAL corresponds to O(GeV) of incident-equivalent energy.
  - +0.175 GeV in a crystal is about +0.175 GeV.
- The "partly canceling" shifts are therefore an artifact of the unweighted sum.
- **Fix, with no metadata needed:**
  - Fit K_inc ≈ a·E_ECAL + b·E_HCAL (+ c) on Geant4 training events.
  - Apply the same weights to both samples.
  - Report linearity and σ(E)/E per energy bin.
  - Remove the 1.0% figure from the abstract.

### B5. The detector is unnamed and the data are collaboration-owned

The geometry matches the ePIC ZDC:
- The ePIC ZDC HCAL is a 64-layer SiPM-on-tile iron sampling calorimeter with 8096 channels (121/132 per layer), with alternate layers shifted by half a cell.
- The Academia Sinica ZDC group describes a crystal ECAL plus a steel/scintillator-tile HCAL with 64 layers, and refers to the ZDC in the ePIC official MC.
- Your centroids sit near x ≈ −0.9 m. That fits a detector positioned at z = 35 m along the hadron beam's crossing-angle direction. This last step is my inference.
- Your 100 channels per layer does not match the current 121/132, which suggests an older geometry version.

Consequences:
- **Naming:** name the detector and state the geometry tag.
- **Clearance:** a single-author paper using a "collaboration-owned" event file generally needs collaboration clearance before submission. Confirm this.
- **Metadata:** "unavailable" to you is not the same as unavailable. Geant4 version, physics list and materials exist in the production-campaign records.

## 2. Major issues

**M1. The energy-bin claim is unsupported.**
- The reference bin means themselves zigzag. Successive increments are +0.49, +0.64, +0.32, +1.02, +0.16, +0.89 and +0.17 GeV.
- The spread of these increments is about 1.8× the generator's.
- The largest generator-minus-reference differences (±7.7%) fall where the reference jumps. That pattern is what reference statistical noise would produce.
- "Agreement… does not imply comparable agreement within each bin" needs intervals, and your bootstrap code already computes them.

**M2. "Unquantified" overstates the uncertainty problem.** Treating the two samples as independent:

```
Gap fraction (binomial): SE = sqrt(.5243·.4757/9907 + .9353·.0647/9858) = 0.0056
  → Δ = 41.1 ± 1.1 pp (95%)
Worst-case variance (Popoviciu, Var ≤ (b−a)²/4):
  last layer L∈[0,64]:  SE(Δ) ≤ 32·sqrt(1/9907 + 1/9858) = 0.46 → +4.42 is ≥ 9.7σ
  gaps G∈[0,63]:        SE(Δ) ≤ 0.45                      → +3.95 is ≥ 8.8σ
```

Sampling noise within the bank is not the open question. Variation across training seeds, thresholds and graphs is. Rewrite the abstract's uncertainty sentence to say so.

**M3. The below-chance condition-only C2ST score is a leakage signature.**

```
H0 SE for AUROC with 3000 vs 3000 scoring rows ≈ sqrt(6001/(12·3000·3000)) = 0.0075
seeds 0.4739 / 0.4624 / 0.4545 → 3.5σ / 5.0σ / 6.1σ below 0.5
```

- **Mechanism:** about 42% of matched pairs are split across partitions (2 × 0.7 × 0.3). Each split pair has identical K_inc and opposite labels, so a partly memorizing classifier mislabels the held-out twin.
- **Effect on other scores:** the same bias affects every feature family that carries information correlated with the condition.
- **Grouped monitor:** it gives 0.893 against your 0.775 for high-level features. That suggests the row-split scores are biased low, though the different banks prevent proof.
- **Action:** remove the row-split battery from the main text and rerun a grouped split on D_dev.
- **"Predeclared" maximum:** say where 0.65 was declared. If it is in your 23 July CBSC-ZDC specification, cite that document with a timestamp.

**M4. The channel sampler is miscalibrated, and its noise is spatially incoherent.**
- The support logits are trained with BCE (marginal inclusion) plus a ranking loss. They are then used as Plackett–Luce weights in Gumbel-top-K at τ = 1.
- Successive sampling with weights e^s does not in general reproduce σ(s) as inclusion probabilities. Conditional-Poisson sampling with odds e^s does, approximately.
- Separately, the Gumbel noise is i.i.d. per channel and there is no event latent. Nothing makes the fluctuations coherent across neighbours or layers. This is a direct mechanism for the within-run fragmentation in B1.
- **Tests:**
  - Plot per-channel occupancy calibration (generator vs reference) conditioned on layer and K bin.
  - Build a null with occupancy matched to the reference marginals and i.i.d. placement noise.

**M5. The conditioning may be incomplete.**
- The four-vector carries no impact position. If it is recorded at calorimeter entry (you say this is unknown), the transverse location is not determined by the condition.
- The generator would then sample from a blurred score map with i.i.d. noise, which again produces fragmentation.
- **Test on reference data alone:** regress the energy-weighted centroid on (u_x, u_y) and compare the residual RMS with the cell size.

**M6. Report the paired table for empty showers.**
- 0.93% empty showers at 50–250 GeV in a deep calorimeter is most likely geometric acceptance, which should be nearly deterministic in direction.
- Report the 2×2 counts: both empty, reference-only, generator-only, neither. This shows directly whether the visibility head learned the acceptance.

**M7. Standard HEP validation is missing.**
- Calibrated linearity and resolution.
- Reconstructed position or angular resolution. This is the key ZDC metric: the design talk ranks angular (position) resolution as more critical than energy resolution.
- Per-hit energy spectrum.
- Radial profiles.
- Per-layer occupancy histograms.
- Full distributions of L, G, m and R rather than means.
- Event displays.
- A reference-vs-reference W₁ baseline, for example by splitting the reference into halves. Without it, W₁ = 58.68 cannot be interpreted.

**M8. There is no timing number.**
- 0.344 s per event is evaluator wall time.
- For comparison, Wojnar's flow-matching ALICE ZN model reports 0.46 ms inference per sample.
- Without generation-only latency and Geant4 CPU time per shower, the intro's cost motivation is unsupported.

**M9. The connectivity metric uses the model's own graph.**
- The HCAL layers are staggered, and ganged centroids can sit between tiles. kNN-8 on such a layout is not physical adjacency.
- Make geometric overlap or shared-edge adjacency primary, and keep kNN as the sensitivity check.

**M10. Novelty and significance are thin.**
- The idea that matching means does not imply matching structure is already the thesis of Kansal et al. [7]. CaloChallenge likewise stresses that showers carry correlations between voxels across several layers.
- Majerz et al. [13] introduce a Jaccard co-activation error, which is conceptually adjacent to your support metrics. Discuss it rather than only citing it.
- With one seed, 4.35% of the data and no baseline, the result reads as "a pilot model with a factorization defect behaves as the factorization predicts."
- Your legacy recurrent HurdleGraph model is the natural contrast if its recurrence runs over layers. Evaluating it on the same gap metrics would turn the diagnosis into a comparison.
