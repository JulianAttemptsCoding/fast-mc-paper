# Primary literature benchmark, revised 2026-09-23

The revision uses papers submitted before November 2025 as scientific and stylistic references. The prose follows their usual order: detector-simulation problem, target representation, model, evaluation population, observable-level results, and bounded interpretation. Claims from other detectors are not transferred to this readout.

- [CaloFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05285) motivates learned calorimeter simulation and evaluates detector observables rather than relying on the training objective.
- [CaloDiffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03876) provides a diffusion and geometry-adaptation precedent.
- [CaloGraph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11575) motivates graph models for irregular calorimeter geometry; it does not validate this project's graph.
- [CaloClouds II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05704) provides a point-cloud precedent for geometry-independent high-granularity simulation.
- [CaloDREAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09629) combines layer-energy modeling with high-dimensional shower generation and evaluates multiple representations.
- [published CaloChallenge 2022](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6633/ae1304) compares one-dimensional observables, physics distances, classifier tests, model size, and generation time. It establishes that multi-view evaluation is existing practice; the manuscript makes no novelty claim for that general principle.
- [Evaluating generative models in HEP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10295) studies the sensitivity of complementary two-sample metrics to different failure modes. It supports avoiding a single scalar fidelity claim.
- [ALICE ZDC VAE/GAN study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13606), [ALICE ZDC diffusion study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03233), [published ALICE ZDC flow-matching study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2025.109936), and [ExpertSim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20991) establish prior ZDC surrogate work across several generative model families. Their target representations and detectors differ from the raw-deposit readout here, so no cross-paper numerical ranking is made.
- The [published 2025 ALICE ZDC comparison](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41781-025-00130-x) establishes a journal version of prior dedicated ZDC surrogate work. The [August 2026 ALICE ZDC normalizing-flow study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12795) evaluates channel co-activation across showers at fixed inputs, a different quantity from within-event graph components; it is cited for current scientific context, not used as a pre-November-2025 prose model. Neither provides a like-for-like numerical baseline for the readout in this manuscript.
- [Geant4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-9002(03)01368-8) is cited as the particle-transport toolkit. Its general capabilities do not fill the missing production-specific material, physics-list, cut, field, or seed metadata.
- [ATLAS topological clustering](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.02934) shows that adjacency and signal thresholds can define physically useful calorimeter objects. It also sharpens the limitation of this manuscript: the reported components use one model graph and a zero threshold, rather than a detector-calibrated clustering definition.
- [ParaFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21461) shows that upstream material can be an explicit simulation condition. The incomplete upstream-material metadata in this project therefore remains a real target-definition limitation.
- [Flow Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747), [Gumbel Top-k](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/kool19a.html), and [classifier two-sample tests](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.06545) support the method descriptions. Project-specific solver, mask, pairing, and split rules are checked against local source rather than attributed to those papers.

The literature does not establish this checkpoint's fidelity, graph robustness, physical threshold, detector identity, reference-simulation configuration, or speed. Those claims require project-specific evidence.
