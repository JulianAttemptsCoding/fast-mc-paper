\title{Longitudinal Gaps and Readout Connectivity in a Pilot\\Generative Model of Zero-Degree Calorimeter Showers}
\author{Julian Juan}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We study a pilot generative model of single-neutron Geant4 showers in a 6,790-channel Zero-Degree Calorimeter readout. Conditioned on neutron energy and direction, the model samples a total readout deposit, active layers, layer energies, active channels, and channel energies. We compare one checkpoint trained on 26,624 events with reference showers at 10,000 stored conditions between 50 and 250\,GeV incident kinetic energy. The mean total deposit differs by 0.045\,GeV (1.0\%), partly because opposite electromagnetic and hadronic shifts cancel. Among nonempty events, mean active-layer counts differ by 0.25, but the generated showers reach 4.42 layers farther downstream and contain 3.95 more inactive layers within their occupied span. Mean active-channel counts differ by $-28.7$ ($-1.8\%$); mean component counts on the model graph differ by $+35.97$. Because that graph links only adjacent layers, inactive layers can themselves split the support. The high-level classifier two-sample test gives AUROC 0.775, above its declared 0.65 limit, although its row-wise split is not a clean held-out-pair test. The comparison is descriptive for one checkpoint on a repeatedly inspected validation sample. The retained aggregates cannot separate longitudinal splitting from within-layer fragmentation or establish the structural difference's uncertainty, threshold dependence, or persistence across fits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Detailed particle transport is central to detector studies, but calorimeter shower simulation is computationally expensive. Generative models based on normalizing flows, diffusion, point clouds, graph networks, and conditional flow matching have been developed for faster sampling~\cite{caloflow,calodiffusion,calograph,caloclouds2,calodream}. CaloChallenge evaluates such models with shower observables, distributional distances, classifier tests, model size, and generation time~\cite{calochallenge}. Different tests can reveal different distributional failures~\cite{kansalmetrics}.

Zero-Degree Calorimeters (ZDCs) have their own fast-simulation literature. Studies of the ALICE ZDC have used variational autoencoders, generative adversarial networks, diffusion models, conditional flow matching, and mixtures of generative experts~\cite{dubinskizdc,kitazdc,fasterzdc,expertsim,wojnar2025}. A recent normalizing-flow study also tests co-activation of channels across showers at fixed incident conditions~\cite{majerz2026}. Those studies use a different detector representation, so their numerical results cannot be compared directly with the raw deposited-energy vector examined here.

Spatial organization matters in calorimetry. ATLAS, for example, clusters neighboring cells above signal-significance thresholds and uses cluster shapes in reconstruction~\cite{atlastopocluster}. Here we examine connectivity on a different, model-defined graph with a zero threshold. Its components are diagnostic properties of the stored readout, not reconstructed shower clusters.

We examine one pilot-trained checkpoint on an irregular 65-layer readout. The 10,000-condition comparison was inspected repeatedly during development, and only its aggregate report is available in the public package. We compare total and section deposits, longitudinal activity, channel occupancy, and graph components to locate disagreements that a total-response mean would hide.

\section{Simulation target and evaluation population}
\label{sec:data}

For each event, the stored condition is an incident-neutron four-vector $p^\mu=(E,p_x,p_y,p_z)$. The model input is
\begin{equation}
 c=\left(\log(1+\Kinc/100\GeV),\,u_x,\,u_y,\,u_z,\,\log(E/\GeV)\right),
 \qquad \mathbf u=\mathbf p/\lVert\mathbf p\rVert,
 \label{eq:condition}
\end{equation}
where $\Kinc=E-m_n$. The two energy features are deterministically related by $E=\Kinc+m_n$; retaining both is a frozen implementation choice and does not add independent conditioning information.

The target $\Y\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{6790}$ contains raw, nondigitized energy deposits summed by valid readout ID from a Geant4 simulation~\cite{geant4}. It is neither the incident-neutron energy nor all energy lost in detector material. Layer 0 contains 400 electromagnetic-calorimeter (ECAL) channels. Hadronic-calorimeter (HCAL) layers 1--63 contain 100 channels each and layer 64 contains 90, for 6,390 HCAL channels. Of the HCAL readout IDs, 2,400 gang two to four stable physical positions. Each ganged channel is represented by the unweighted centroid of its positions (Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}). The released metadata do not specify the full material composition, Geant4 version, physics list, production cuts, field configuration, or whether $p^\mu$ is recorded at generation or calorimeter entry. The few-GeV readout totals are therefore reported as deposits in this stored target, without interpreting their ratio to incident energy as a calibrated response.

The graph connects every channel to its eight nearest same-layer centroids. For each node in layer $\ell=0,\ldots,63$, four nearest centroids are selected in layer $\ell+1$, and each selected longitudinal pair is stored in both directions. This gives $8\times6790=54{,}320$ lateral directed edges and $2\times4\times(400+63\times100)=53{,}600$ longitudinal directed edges, or 107,920 in total. The same graph is used by the generator and by the connectivity diagnostic. It is an implementation graph, not a uniquely established physical-neighbor graph, and centroiding removes separation within a ganged channel.

The preparation record contains 764,940 events over 0--300\,GeV, divided by deterministic event hash into 612,482 training, 76,158 validation, and 76,300 nominal test events. The checkpoint studied here used a smaller pilot of 26,624 training events and 6,656 validation events. Its training population is 4.35\% of the canonical training partition.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
All shower comparisons use a 10,000-condition sample from the canonical validation partition with $50\leq\Kinc\leq250\GeV$. We call this sample $\Ddev$. It was inspected during model and manuscript development, and its overlap with the 6,656-event pilot-validation sample is not recoverable from the released aggregates. The pilot and canonical split manifests have different hashes because they describe different partitions; the recorded preparation cross-tabulation places pilot training events within canonical training and pilot validation events within canonical validation. No nominal test event is used in this manuscript. The stored comparisons are development-sample measurements, not an untouched test of expected performance.

\section{Generator and training procedure}
\label{sec:model}

The generator has 2,082,507 trainable parameters. At generation it receives only the five features in Eq.~\eqref{eq:condition}. A two-block residual multilayer perceptron maps them to a 128-component representation $h_c$, which conditions each sampling stage. The discrete stages predict probability distributions for visibility, layer activity, and channel counts or selection. Two conditional flow-matching models transform independent Gaussian draws into continuous layer-energy and channel-energy allocations. The model has no learned shower encoder or common random latent vector: independent draws enter at the discrete heads and at the two flows.

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} follows the sampling path. Each draw conditions later stages. Thus a change in total deposit can affect every later allocation, whereas the final channel-energy assignment cannot change a previously sampled layer budget or channel count.

\subsection{Total deposit and longitudinal allocation}

A Bernoulli head draws a provisional visibility flag $V_0$, and a second head specifies a four-component Gaussian mixture in transformed total deposit. With $\sigma$ the logistic function and $\pi_m$ softmax-normalized mixture weights, the two draws use
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr(V_0=1\mid h_c)&=\sigma(a_\theta(h_c)),
 &q_T&=\log(1+T/10\GeV),\\
 p_\theta(q\mid h_c)&=\sum_{m=1}^{4}\pi_m(h_c)
 \mathcal N\!\left(q;\mu_m(h_c),\sigma_m^2(h_c)\right).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:response_transform}
\end{equation}
The mixture widths are positive by construction. For a draw $q$, the implementation inverts the transform, clips at zero and at the training-derived cap, and defines the final response as
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 C(\Kinc)&=\min\{0.630110\max(\Kinc,1\GeV),61.2338\GeV\},\\
 T_{\mathrm{cand}}&=\min\{10\GeV\max(e^q-1,0),C(\Kinc)\},\\
 V&=V_0\,\mathbf 1_{\Kinc>0}\,\mathbf 1_{T_{\mathrm{cand}}>0},
 \qquad T=VT_{\mathrm{cand}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:cap}
\end{equation}
The cap becomes constant at 61.2338\,GeV for $\Kinc\gtrsim97.2\GeV$. Clipping a negative inverse-transform value sets $V=0$, so no layer is activated. The aggregate evaluation did not retain cap-activation or reference-exceedance counts, so the response tail near this boundary is untested.

For $V=1$, a categorical head samples the first active layer, $F\sim\mathrm{Cat}[\operatorname{softmax}f_\theta(h_c,\log(1+T/\GeV))]$. Layers before $F$ are set inactive, layer $F$ is forced active, and a Bernoulli head samples the later activity indicators. These later draws are independent conditional on their inputs; the factorization cannot represent arbitrary conditional correlations between layer-activity patterns:
\begin{equation}
 p(\mathbf A\mid h_c,T,F)=\prod_{\ell>F}^{64}p(A_\ell\mid h_c,T,F,\ell),
 \qquad A_F=1.
 \label{eq:activity}
\end{equation}
This head does not require the active layers to be contiguous.

The first conditional flow apportions $T$ among active layers. A star denotes a quantity derived from a reference shower. For a nonempty reference shower, its target is a centered log layer fraction, with inactive coordinates zero:
\begin{equation}
 a_\ell^\star=\log\max\!\left(
 \frac{B_\ell^\star}{\max(T^\star,10^{-12}\GeV)},10^{-8}\right),
 \quad (x_1)_\ell=A_\ell^\star\left(
 a_\ell^\star-\frac{\sum_m A_m^\star a_m^\star}
 {\max(1,\sum_m A_m^\star)}\right).
 \label{eq:profile_target}
\end{equation}
For an empty reference shower, the target and mask are zero.
For each training example, $x_0$ is a standard-normal draw on active coordinates and $t$ is uniform on $[0,1]$. With $M$ the active-coordinate mask, the straight-path flow-matching objective is
\begin{equation}
 x_t=(1-t)x_0+t x_1,\qquad
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}=
 \frac{\sum_{b,j}M_{bj}
 [v_\theta(x_t,t;\mathrm{ctx})_{bj}-(x_1-x_0)_{bj}]^2}
 {\max(1,\sum_{b,j}M_{bj})}.
 \label{eq:cfm}
\end{equation}
Here $b$ indexes examples in a minibatch and $j$ indexes layer or channel coordinates. The squared error trains the velocity field toward $x_1-x_0$ on the active coordinates~\cite{flowmatching}. Both flows use the same eight-step generation rule, with $M$ set to the active-layer or selected-channel mask:
\begin{equation}
 z_0=M\odot\varepsilon,\quad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I),\qquad
 z_{n+1}=M\odot\left[z_n+\tfrac18
 v_\theta\!\left(z_n,\tfrac{n+1/2}{8};\mathrm{ctx}\right)\right],
 \quad n=0,\ldots,7.
 \label{eq:flow_steps}
\end{equation}
The velocity is evaluated at the midpoint \emph{time} and the current state $z_n$. For a visible event, a masked softmax converts the final layer state to fractions. The layer budgets are
\begin{equation}
 \pi_\ell=\frac{A_\ell e^{z_{8,\ell}}}
 {\sum_{m:A_m=1}e^{z_{8,m}}},\qquad
 B_\ell=T\pi_\ell,\qquad \sum_{\ell=0}^{64}B_\ell=T.
 \label{eq:layer_budget}
\end{equation}
The velocity field uses a 128-component layer representation and two bidirectional transformer layers with four attention heads. Its context comprises $h_c$, $T$, the sampled activity mask, layer identity, and a sinusoidal encoding of flow time.

\subsection{Channel count, support, and deposited energy}

For each layer, a categorical head receives $(h_c,B_\ell,A_\ell)$ and a learned layer identifier, then samples the active-channel count $K_\ell$. Its feasibility mask forces $K_\ell=0$ in an inactive layer and $1\leq K_\ell\leq N_\ell$ in an active layer, where $N_\ell$ is that layer's channel count. The mask fixes a count but imposes no minimum energy per channel in this zero-threshold study.

The support network scores every channel for selection. Its initial channel features are standardized $(x,y,z)$ coordinates, normalized layer index, ECAL/HCAL indicators, $h_c$, the layer budget $B_\ell$, and the requested occupancy fraction $K_\ell/N_\ell$. Three 96-component graph blocks pass messages along the frozen directed edges. Writing $e_{ji}$ for the five edge inputs (coordinate differences, distance, and edge type), a block has the form
\begin{equation}
 m_i^{(r)}=\sum_{j:(j,i)\in E}
 \phi_r(h_j^{(r)},h_i^{(r)},e_{ji}),\qquad
 h_i^{(r+1)}=h_i^{(r)}+\psi_r(h_i^{(r)},m_i^{(r)}).
 \label{eq:message}
\end{equation}
Channel representations are then averaged within each layer. A two-layer, four-head bidirectional transformer exchanges information among the 65 layer summaries, and its output is broadcast back to the channels before scoring. For scores $s_{\ell i}$, the sampled support is
\begin{equation}
 g_{\ell i}=-\log[-\log U_{\ell i}],\quad
 U_{\ell i}\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1),\qquad
 S_\ell=\operatorname{TopK}_{K_\ell}
 \{s_{\ell i}/\tau+g_{\ell i}:i\in\mathcal N_\ell\},
 \label{eq:topk}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal N_\ell$ is the set of valid channels in layer $\ell$ and $\tau>0$ is the fixed sampling temperature~\cite{gumbeltopk}. The implementation clips uniform draws away from zero and one before taking logarithms. Independent Gumbel perturbations and top-$K_\ell$ selection enforce the count, but not connectivity.

The second conditional flow divides each sampled layer budget among its selected channels. For a reference layer with $K_\ell^\star>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 a_{\ell i}^\star=\log\max\!\left(
 \frac{Y_{\ell i}^\star}{\max(B_\ell^\star,10^{-12}\GeV)},10^{-8}\right),\qquad
 (x_1)_{\ell i}=\mathbf 1_{i\in S_\ell^\star}
 \left(a_{\ell i}^\star-\frac{1}{K_\ell^\star}
 \sum_{j\in S_\ell^\star}a_{\ell j}^\star\right).
 \label{eq:share_target}
\end{equation}
Inactive layers have zero target coordinates. This flow uses the masked objective and update in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:cfm} and \eqref{eq:flow_steps}, now with the reference or sampled channel set as the mask. Its velocity network has the support network's graph and layer-context structure, with the current flow state and a sinusoidal time encoding added at each channel. Unselected channels are masked before and after every graph block. At generation, the eight updates produce logits $r_{\ell i}$ on selected channels. The deterministic decoder computes
\begin{equation}
 Y_{\ell i}=
 \begin{cases}
 B_\ell\,\dfrac{\exp r_{\ell i}}{\sum_{j\in S_\ell}\exp r_{\ell j}}, & i\in S_\ell,\\[6pt]
 0, & i\notin S_\ell,
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:decoder}
\end{equation}
where $S_\ell$ is the selected set. Thus, in exact arithmetic, $|S_\ell|=K_\ell$, $Y_{\ell i}\geq0$, $\sum_iY_{\ell i}=B_\ell$, and $\sum_{\ell i}Y_{\ell i}=T$. These identities conserve the model's sampled readout budget. They do not enforce conservation of incident-neutron energy, agreement with Geant4, or connectivity of the selected support.

\subsection{Training and checkpoint selection}

Training derives $V$, $T$, $F$, $A_\ell$, $B_\ell$, $K_\ell$, $S_\ell$, and both flow targets from each reference shower. Each stage receives reference values from preceding stages; generation instead passes sampled values downstream. This is teacher forcing. The weighted joint objective contains nine component losses:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L_{\rm joint}={}&w_V\mathcal L_{\rm BCE}(V)
 +w_T\mathcal L_{\rm NLL}(T)
 +w_F\mathcal L_{\rm CE}(F)
 +w_A\mathcal L_{\rm BCE}(\mathbf A)
 +w_B\mathcal L_{\rm FM}^{B}\\
 &+w_K\mathcal L_{\rm CE}(\mathbf K)
 +w_S\mathcal L_{\rm BCE}(\mathbf S)
 +w_R\mathcal L_{\rm rank}(\mathbf S)
 +w_Y\mathcal L_{\rm FM}^{Y}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:joint_loss}
\end{equation}
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
The response NLL includes the Jacobian $1/(T+10\GeV)$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:response_transform}. The support-ranking term averages $\log[1+\exp(-(s_i-s_j))]$ over sampled occupied--unoccupied channel pairs. Superscripts $B$ and $Y$ name the layer-budget and channel-share flow losses; both use the masked squared error in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cfm}. The count loss downweights inactive-layer zero counts. Weights $w_j$ were obtained from gradient norms measured on training data. The response, layer-profile, and count weights reached the lower clipping bound, and the visibility weight reached the upper bound. These weights describe the optimization; they do not identify the source of the connectivity discrepancy.

The selected checkpoint's training lineage used AdamW with a peak learning rate of $3\times10^{-4}$, batch size six, accumulation over four batches, gradient clipping at one, and seed 20260723. Epoch 90 has the lowest recorded validation objective among the 104 retained rows spanning epochs 11--114 and defines the checkpoint studied here. Validation objectives are lower than training objectives over much of this record. The retained aggregates do not identify why, so the ordering is not given a physical interpretation. Both flows use eight integration updates at evaluation; no solver-step comparison was retained.

\section{Diagnostics}
\label{sec:diagnostics}

We compare mean total, ECAL, and HCAL deposits over all events. For eight 25\,GeV incident-energy bins, the report retains event counts, means, and standard deviations, but no confidence intervals for generated-minus-reference bin differences. A paired, incident-energy-stratified bootstrap with 1,000 resamples supplies 95\% percentile intervals for the zero-deposit-fraction difference and the hit-count Wasserstein distance. It does not supply intervals for the gap or component metrics.

A layer or channel is active when its stored energy is strictly positive; no detector or analysis threshold is applied. For a nonempty event with first and last active layers $F$ and $L$, the occupied span is $L-F+1$, and the number of inactive layers inside it is
\begin{equation}
 G=L-F+1-\sum_{\ell=0}^{64}A_\ell.
 \label{eq:gaps}
\end{equation}
This counts inactive layers, not contiguous runs. A shower has an interior gap when $G>0$. Ignoring edge direction, the occupied channels $S$ form connected components $C_1,\ldots,C_m$ on the model graph. We report their number $m$ and the fraction of occupied channels in the largest one, $\max_j|C_j|/|S|$. These support summaries use only nonempty events: 9,907 reference and 9,858 generated events. Because longitudinal graph edges connect adjacent layers only, a contiguous run of inactive layers between occupied layers necessarily separates the support on either side. The retained value of $G$ cannot tell us how many such runs occur.

\Needspace{5\baselineskip}
The original classifier two-sample-test (C2ST) battery uses three classifier seeds and a row-wise random train/test split of labelled reference and generated showers. Members of a matched condition pair can fall on opposite sides of that split, so these are screening scores rather than a clean held-out-pair fidelity test. Its condition-only AUROC is 0.464. A separate pair-grouped validation monitor records condition-only AUROC 0.500 and high-level AUROC 0.893 on its own event bank. The two batteries are not interchangeable; the condition-only scores test the classifier pipeline, not shower fidelity. A future classifier test should hold out matched pairs together~\cite{c2st,kansalmetrics}.

At channel level, the generated sample has 28.7 fewer active channels and 35.97 more weak components on average. Its mean largest-component fraction is lower by 6.03 percentage points. These component statistics describe the same strict-positive support that contains the additional longitudinal gaps. The aggregate report does not distinguish components separated by empty layers from those arising within occupied layers, nor does it record component energies. A small positive deposit counts as occupied under this definition even if it would be removed by a realistic threshold.

The all-event hit-count Wasserstein distance is 58.68 channels, with a stored paired-bootstrap 95\% interval of 51.47--66.92 channels. This distributional discrepancy accompanies the smaller difference in mean active-channel count; the interval describes resampling of the inspected bank.

The original C2ST battery records AUROC 0.775 for high-level shower summaries, 0.791 for lower-level features, and 0.856 for layer-profile features, each averaged over three classifier seeds. Its high-level value exceeds the predeclared maximum of 0.65. These scores are evidence of separability under the recorded split, with the pairing caveat above; they are neither an independent test result nor a calibrated measure of simulation fidelity.

The mean fraction of directed graph edges with both endpoints occupied is 0.1375 for generated showers and 0.1458 for the reference, averaged over all 10,000 events. This local co-occupancy statistic is subject to the same graph and threshold choices as the component counts.

There are 49 more zero-deposit generated events than reference events, a difference of 0.49 percentage points. The stored paired bootstrap gives a nominal 95\% percentile interval of 0.19--0.76 percentage points for this difference. Because the same validation sample was repeatedly inspected, this interval describes resampling variation within the selected bank; it is not a confirmatory coverage statement for a new sample. Empty events are excluded from the support statistics and therefore change their denominators.

The decoder-level checks passed for all 1,250 batches of eight generated events. No nonfinite value, negative value, invalid support, requested-versus-realized count mismatch, or positive-support mismatch was recorded. Maximum event and layer budget residuals were $1.14\times10^{-5}$ and $3.05\times10^{-5}$\,GeV under the evaluator's batch-wide absolute-plus-relative tolerance. These checks establish consistency with sampled budgets and counts for this generated bank; they do not test agreement with Geant4.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The longitudinal summaries show that generated showers extend farther downstream and contain more inactive layers between their first and last deposits. The graph-component difference is partly entangled with that result by construction. A contiguous empty-layer run separates occupied layers on an adjacent-layer graph, while additional components may also occur within occupied layers. The retained aggregates count empty layers but neither their runs nor the locations and energies of individual components. They therefore do not establish an independent lateral-fragmentation effect.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
The model enforces layer budgets and active-channel counts, not adjacency among selected channels. Its independent later-layer activity draws and Gumbel-perturbed top-$K_\ell$ support selection could permit gaps and fragmented support. This is a mechanism the architecture allows, not an explanation isolated by the present comparison. Pilot training size and validation-objective checkpoint selection remain untested contributors. The layer-budget flow can affect later counts and support through its budgets; the final channel-energy flow cannot change sampled layer activity or selected channel sets in exact arithmetic. Solver-step effects on energy allocations were not tested. A matched ablation or an oracle cascade replacing one generated upstream variable at a time with its reference value could locate where the discrepancy first appears.

The strict-positive support includes arbitrarily small stored deposits; it is not a thresholded detector observable. Recomputing component energies and connectivity under physically motivated energy thresholds, alternative adjacency graphs, and fixed energy and direction slices is necessary before interpreting the difference as shower morphology.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
$\Ddev$ was repeatedly inspected, its overlap with pilot validation is unresolved, and the public package contains aggregates rather than paired event arrays. It cannot provide structural uncertainty intervals or the threshold and graph scans above. Repeated generation of a fixed checkpoint would measure sampling variation; nearby checkpoints and at least three independently trained final seeds would test fit variation. A locked bank, complete simulation provenance, reconstructed-observable comparisons, and end-to-end timing would be needed to assess use as fast simulation. These measurements describe the stored checkpoint and bank.

\section{Conclusion}

For this pilot checkpoint, similar mean occupancies accompany a later, more interrupted longitudinal support in the stored validation comparison: mean active layers differ by $+0.25$, mean last active layer by $+4.42$, and mean inactive layers within the occupied span by $+3.95$. The generated sample has 35.97 more weak components on the model graph, but the adjacent-layer graph makes this statistic inseparable from longitudinal gaps without event-level analysis. The mean total deposit differs by only 0.045\,GeV because the ECAL and HCAL shifts partly cancel. These findings identify where the present surrogate disagrees with its reference sample; they do not validate a fast-simulation replacement or identify a causal model defect.
